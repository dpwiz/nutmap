The phrase kuri-iro evokes the warm brown of the nut, and the fruit is tied to the traditional seasonal eating culture of aki.

### Historian

The chestnut emoji depicts the glossy brown nut still partly wrapped in its spiky husk, a food that has nourished people since prehistory. The English word traces back through Middle English 'chesteine' and Old French 'chastaigne' to Latin 'castanea' and Greek 'kastanon', likely borrowed from an Anatolian region near Castana. Sweet chestnuts were a staple flour crop across Mediterranean Europe for centuries, earning the tree the nickname 'bread tree', before potatoes and maize displaced them. Roasting chestnuts became a winter street tradition in cities like Paris and London, which is why the nut is strongly tied to cold-season memories.

### Scale and physics

Viewed through scale and physics, a chestnut is a dense, roughly centimetre-to-few-centimetre nut with a hard shell that protects a starchy kernel, typically weighing around 5 to 15 grams. Its size places it between a pea and a golf ball, and it is many millions of times larger than a single atom but tiny compared with a tree or a planet. When dropped from a tree, a chestnut falls at terminal velocity of only a few metres per second because of its small mass and air resistance, and its hard husk absorbs the impact energy. Its seed-dispersal behaviour relies on gravity, and the spiky burr casing can explode open from the energy stored in the drying tissue.
